Key Findings

Weather affects prices. Each additional mm of rainfall is associated with approximately 0.25% lower food prices, and each additional °C with approximately 1.29% lower prices (both p < 0.001), controlling for commodity, district, and year.
Commodity prices differ significantly. Prices vary significantly across commodities (ANOVA F = 4,074, p < 0.001) — maize, rice, cassava, and beans have fundamentally different market dynamics.
Season does not significantly affect prices. Wet vs. dry season price difference is not statistically significant (p = 0.616).
District differences are not significant. Price differences across districts are not statistically significant (p = 0.460) after controlling for commodity.

Recommendations

  1. Collect district-level weather data rather than using Kampala-only weather for all districts.
  2. Use robust standard errors (e.g., HC3) to address heteroscedasticity in regression inference.
  3. Investigate pre-2015 WFP data if the project scope is extended beyond 2015–2024.
  4. Add more districts to the canonical district list as WFP expands coverage.
  5. Consider time-series models (e.g., SARIMA) to capture seasonal price cycles more explicitly.
